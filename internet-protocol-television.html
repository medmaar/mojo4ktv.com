Definition

What is Internet Protocol Television?

Internet Protocol Television — universally abbreviated as IPTV — is a method of delivering television content using the Internet Protocol (IP) suite, the same technology that powers the internet itself. Instead of broadcasting content over radio frequencies (terrestrial TV), coaxial cables (cable TV), or satellite signals, IPTV transmits television as data packets over any IP network.

The term "Internet Protocol Television" was formally defined by the ITU (International Telecommunication Union) and covers three distinct services: live television broadcasting, video on demand (VOD), and time-shifted television (catch-up TV). All three are delivered over IP networks — typically the public internet via broadband, or private managed networks operated by telecoms companies.

IPTV is not a new technology. Major telecoms operators (AT&T U-verse, BT TV, Orange TV) have operated managed IPTV networks since the 2000s. What has changed is the quality and reliability of over-the-top (OTT) IPTV services delivered via the public internet — making IPTV accessible to anyone with a broadband connection, without a telecoms provider subscription.

How Internet Protocol Television Works — The Technology

Understanding how IPTV works requires understanding the difference between broadcasting and unicasting:

Traditional TV: Broadcasting

Cable, satellite and terrestrial TV broadcast all channels simultaneously to all receivers. Every home receives every channel at all times. The TV tuner selects which channel to display. Highly inefficient — bandwidth is used for all 500 channels even when you watch one.

Internet Protocol Television: Unicasting

IPTV sends only the channel you are watching to your device. When you change channels, a new unicast stream starts. Bandwidth is used only for what you actually watch. This is why IPTV can deliver 50,000+ channels without requiring 50,000 simultaneous broadcasts.

When you select a channel in an IPTV app like TiviMate, the app sends a request to the IPTV server. The server encodes the broadcast signal as H.264 or H.265 video, wraps it in IP packets, and streams it over the internet to your device. Your device's IPTV app decodes the packets and plays the video — with a typical delay of 2-5 seconds compared to traditional broadcast.

For live sports and news, this 2-5 second delay is imperceptible in normal use. It only becomes relevant if you are following real-time social media commentary simultaneously — in which case the IPTV stream will appear slightly behind.

Internet Protocol Television Protocols — Technical Overview

H.264 / H.265 (HEVC)

The video compression codecs used for most IPTV streams. H.265 (HEVC) delivers the same quality as H.264 at half the bitrate — critical for 4K streaming at acceptable bandwidth requirements. Most modern IPTV devices support both.

HLS & MPEG-TS

The two stream delivery formats. HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) breaks streams into small segments for adaptive bitrate delivery. MPEG-TS (MPEG Transport Stream) is the traditional broadcast-style format. TiviMate supports both — try switching if you experience buffering.

Xtream Codes API

The de facto standard API for IPTV subscription management. Provides channel list, EPG data, and VOD access through a server URL, username, and password. Used by TiviMate, IPTV Smarters, and MyTV Online. Mojo 4K uses Xtream Codes for all subscriptions.

M3U / M3U_plus

A playlist format that lists channel stream URLs with metadata (logos, categories, EPG IDs). M3U_plus extends basic M3U with additional fields for IPTV-specific data. Works in VLC, Kodi, Smart IPTV, and any M3U-compatible player.

Internet Protocol Television — Frequently Asked Questions

What is Internet Protocol Television?

Internet Protocol Television (IPTV) is the delivery of television content over IP networks — typically broadband internet — rather than cable, satellite, or terrestrial broadcast signals. It enables live TV, video on demand, and catch-up television on any internet-connected device.

What are the three types of Internet Protocol Television?

The three types are: (1) Live IPTV — real-time live channels including sports, news and entertainment; (2) Video on Demand (VOD) — on-demand movies and series; (3) Time-shifted TV — catch-up functionality allowing replay of past broadcasts, typically for 7 days.

How does IPTV differ from cable TV?

Cable TV broadcasts all channels simultaneously over a fixed cable. IPTV sends only your selected channel as a stream over your broadband.
