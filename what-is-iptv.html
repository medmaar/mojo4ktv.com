DEFINITION

IPTV (Internet Protocol Television) is a method of delivering television content over the internet instead of through traditional cable, satellite, or antenna signals. Rather than receiving broadcasts through a physical cable or dish, IPTV streams video content via your broadband internet connection, allowing you to watch live TV, movies, and on-demand content on any device — including smart TVs, phones, tablets, and streaming sticks.

How Does IPTV Work?

IPTV works by converting TV signals into data packets and transmitting them over the internet using the same IP (Internet Protocol) technology that powers websites and streaming services like Netflix. When you play an IPTV channel, your device sends a request to the IPTV provider's server, which streams the content back in real-time.

IPTV technology is built on the same Internet Protocol (RFC 791) that powers the web. The Ofcom Connected Nations report confirms that broadband speeds in the UK now comfortably support 4K IPTV streaming for the vast majority of households.

How Does an IPTV Subscription Deliver Streams?

An IPTV service like Mojo 4K operates a network of high-performance media servers. When you subscribe, you receive login credentials (username, password, and server URL). You enter these into an IPTV player app — such as IPTV Smarters Pro or TiviMate — on your device. The app connects to the server and streams content directly to your screen in real time.

The content is delivered using the Xtream Codes or M3U playlist protocol, which is the industry standard for IPTV streaming services. Premium providers like Mojo 4K use dedicated, load-balanced server infrastructure to maintain high picture quality and low buffering even when millions of viewers are watching simultaneously.

Is IPTV Legal?

A Brief History of IPTV

IPTV technology emerged in the mid-1990s as telecoms companies began experimenting with delivering television signals over broadband internet connections. The first commercial IPTV service launched in 1999, initially serving a small number of households with a handful of channels at low resolution. By 2010, major telecoms providers in Europe and North America had rolled out IPTV as part of triple-play packages (internet, TV, phone).

The modern IPTV era began around 2015 when dedicated IPTV subscription services emerged, offering channel lists far exceeding what any single pay-TV provider could offer. Technological improvements in compression (HEVC/H.265 codec) made HD and then 4K streaming viable over standard broadband connections, and the proliferation of fast 4G and fibre internet meant that by 2020, IPTV had become the default choice for millions of cord-cutters worldwide.

Types of IPTV Streams Explained

IPTV services deliver content through three distinct stream types, each serving a different viewing pattern. Live IPTV streams broadcast content in real-time — exactly like traditional TV — and is what accounts for the 50,000+ live channels in a service like Mojo 4K. Time-shifted IPTV (also called catch-up TV) allows you to watch content that was broadcast in the past 24–72 hours, stored on the provider's servers. Video on Demand (VOD) is a library of pre-recorded content you can watch at any time — Mojo 4K includes 300,000+ VOD titles.

The protocol used for live IPTV is typically UDP multicast (for network efficiency) or HTTP Live Streaming (HLS) for internet delivery. Most consumer IPTV apps including TiviMate, IPTV Smarters Pro, and GSE Smart IPTV handle all three stream types through a single subscription connection.

IPTV vs Satellite vs Cable vs Streaming

IPTV differs from satellite TV (which requires a dish and receiver), cable TV (which requires a coaxial cable connection to your home), and OTT streaming services (Netflix, Disney+, which only offer on-demand content). IPTV is the only technology that combines live TV, catch-up, and on-demand content across any internet-connected device — making it the most versatile and cost-effective television solution available in 2026.

The technical advantage of IPTV over satellite is the absence of weather interference and the ability to stream on any device. The advantage over cable is price (no infrastructure cost), portability (works anywhere with internet), and channel variety (no geographic licensing restrictions).

Legal Considerations for IPTV in 2026

The legality of IPTV depends entirely on the provider. Licensed IPTV services — those that have negotiated content rights with broadcasters — are fully legal. Mojo 4K operates as a premium licensed IPTV service. Unlicensed "free IPTV" services that stream copyrighted content without authorization are illegal in most jurisdictions. When evaluating any IPTV provider, the presence of a proper subscription model, customer support, and clear terms of service are indicators of a legitimate operation.
